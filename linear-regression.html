data_dir <- "/courses/EDS222/data"

# Approximate location of the former Exide facility (2700 S Indiana St, Vernon)
exide_lon <- -118.1923
exide_lat <- 34.0058

# Load soil cores, drop extreme outliers, and calculate distance to Exide.
# distGeo() returns meters, so divide by 1000 to get km.
soil_cores <- read_csv(file.path(data_dir, "exide-soil-cores.csv")) |>
  filter(pb_ppm <= 5e3) |>
  mutate(
    area = str_remove(str_sub(property_id, 1, 3), "-"),

      c(exide_lon, exide_lat)

# Average the soil cores within each property, then join the covariates

  group_by(property_id, area) |>
  summarize(
    pb_ppm = mean(pb_ppm),
    dist_exide_km = mean(dist_exide_km),
    .groups = "drop"
  ) |>
  left_join(property_covariates, by = "property_id") |>
  mutate(
    home_age = 2026 - year_built,
    dist_freeway_km = dist_to_freeway_m / 1000
  )

1. Simpson’s paradox

Scenario

For decades, cars burned leaded gasoline, and lead from tailpipe exhaust settled into nearby soil. A resident asks:

Is soil lead higher at properties closer to a freeway?

You will investigate this question using properties in the ENA and ESA sampling areas only. As you’ll see, the answer depends on whether you account for sampling area. This is an example of Simpson’s paradox: a relationship between two variables that changes direction (or disappears) when a third variable is taken into account.

2. Fit and interpret an interactive model

Scenario

Exide emitted lead into the air for decades, and that lead settled into the soil of surrounding neighborhoods. You might expect soil lead to decline with distance from the facility. However, the three sampling areas lie in different directions from the facility and have different street layouts and land uses. Airborne emissions are carried by prevailing winds, so the rate at which soil lead declines with distance may not be the same in every area.

Older homes are also more likely to have been painted with lead-based paint, which is an important source of soil lead independent of Exide. To isolate the effect of distance from Exide, you’ll need to account for home age.

Your question is:

Does the relationship between soil lead and distance from Exide differ among sampling areas, after accounting for home age?

Answering this question requires an interaction between distance from Exide and sampling area. An additive model (no interaction) forces every area to have the same slope, so it can’t tell you whether the slopes differ.

2b. Write the model

The model you’ll fit has two continuous predictors (dist_exide_km and home_age), one categorical predictor with three levels (area), and an interaction between dist_exide_km and area. Because area has three levels, it needs two dummy variables. ENA is the reference level.

  • \(\text{ESA} = 1\) if the property is in ESA, 0 otherwise
  • \(\text{SS} = 1\) if the property is in SS, 0 otherwise

Here’s the model equation.

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Pb} = \beta_0 &+ \beta_1 \text{dist} + \beta_2 \text{ESA} + \beta_3 \text{SS} + \beta_4 \text{age} \\ &+ \beta_5 \text{dist} \times \text{ESA} + \beta_6 \text{dist} \times \text{SS} + \varepsilon \end{aligned} \]

Now use your equation to fill in the table. Each cell should be written in terms of \(\beta\)s. The first row is done for you.

Tip

Plug in the values of the dummy variables for each area, then collect the terms that are multiplied by \(\text{dist}\) (those make up the slope) and the terms that aren’t (those make up the intercept). Hold age at zero for the intercept.

ESA \(\beta_0\) + \(\beta_2\) \(\beta_1\) + \(\beta_5\)
SS \(\beta_0\) + \(\beta_3\) \(\beta_1\) + \(\beta_6\)

3. Simulate data for an interactive model

Scenario

Each property in the Exide data has soil cores collected from two locations: the drip line (DL, the soil directly under the roof edge) and the yard (YD). These locations get lead from different sources.

  • Yard soil receives lead mostly from airborne deposition, so you’d expect yard soil lead to decline steeply with distance from Exide.
  • Drip line soil also receives lead from paint that flakes off the house and washes off the roof. Paint lead doesn’t depend on distance from Exide, so you’d expect drip line soil to have higher lead overall and to decline less steeply with distance.

In this problem, you’ll simulate data that matches this scenario, fit an interactive model to it, and check how well the model recovers the parameters you used.

The model is:

\[ \text{Pb} = \beta_0 + \beta_1 \times \text{dist} + \beta_2 \times \text{YD} + \beta_3 \times \text{dist} \times \text{YD} + \varepsilon \]

\[ \varepsilon \sim N(0, \sigma) \]

where \(\text{YD} = 1\) for yard samples and 0 for drip line samples. Drip line is the reference level, which matches how R will code it (“DL” comes before “YD” alphabetically).
